THE ARTIST / THE MAKING

Nature, held in metal.

Meet the hands and the ideas behind the work.

ARTIST STATEMENT

Quiet balance between chaos and simplicity.

I make one-of-a-kind jewelry inspired by the natural world: the surface of ancient rock, the shapes left by wind and water, and the small discoveries that happen outdoors. Modern architecture is another influence, bringing clean lines and bold forms into conversation with organic textures.

My mother and grandfather were both artists, and my mother encouraged me to make art from an early age. I began with painting before finding my way to metal. I’ve developed my practice through workshops, experimentation, and many hours at the bench.

I work primarily in sterling silver, copper, and brass, sometimes bringing in vitreous enamel, stones, or pearls. I’m drawn to the marks that form during making. They give a piece a sense of history, even when it is new.

Each piece is individually made, with room for the material to surprise me. My hope is that you find something in it that feels personal to you.

— Deb Collins

AT THE BENCH

How a piece takes shape.

Ideas often begin with an observed line or texture. From there, I cut, shape, join, and work the surface by hand. These techniques let a piece grow beyond the first sketch.

01 / FORM

Cut & shape

Sawing, forging, hammering, and fold forming establish the structure and movement of a piece.

02 / JOIN

Build & set

Soldering and hand fabrication join the elements; a bezel or setting may hold a stone or pearl.

03 / SURFACE

Texture & color

Reticulation, chasing, patina, and enamel create surfaces with depth, contrast, and evidence of the hand.

04 / FINISH

Refine

Edges, connections, balance, and finish are considered until the piece feels right to wear and hold.
